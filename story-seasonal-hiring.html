High-volume operations

300 roles in six weeks, with a hard deadline

In seasonal and high-volume operations hiring, the constraint is not judgement — it is throughput. Every day a shortlist sits unmade is a day closer to shifts that cannot be covered.

The situation

A customer operations function needs 300 people in six weeks to cover a seasonal peak — contact centre, fulfilment and front-line service roles. Around 2,400 people apply.

The roles are not complicated to assess. What matters is communication, reliability, a handful of situational judgement calls and genuine availability for the shift pattern. But at this volume the recruitment team spends its days on the phone establishing facts that a structured conversation could establish without them — and the best candidates, who are applying to three employers at once, accept whoever comes back first.

Speed is the whole game. A shortlist that is accurate but arrives ten days late loses to a shortlist that is good enough and arrives tomorrow.

How the role is set up

Because the roles repeat, the setup happens once and runs for the whole campaign.

  • One competency framework per role family, reviewed and signed off by the operations managers who will actually receive these hires.
  • Interviews trigger on application — candidates are invited immediately and complete the conversation on their own phone, at whatever hour suits shift workers.
  • Availability and right-to-work questions sit inside the same conversation, so hard knock-outs surface before a recruiter spends time on anyone.
  • Scorecards land within hours, ranked, so the team works a live queue rather than a backlog.
At 2,400 applicants, the difference between a same-day shortlist and a ten-day shortlist is the difference between covering the peak and not.

What changes for the team

  • The queue never backs up. Applications are assessed as they arrive rather than in weekly batches, so the team is always working the current pool.
  • Drop-offs cost nothing. In high-volume hiring a large share of invited candidates never start; credits reserve on invite and only settle on completion.
  • Operations managers get evidence they trust. Front-line managers are usually the most sceptical audience for AI scoring — a linked transcript is what converts them.
  • Recruiters move to the offer stage, where speed of human contact genuinely wins candidates.

Where this shape of problem shows up

Retail and hospitality peaks, contact centre ramps, logistics and fulfilment seasons, field service, and any campaign where one role profile is hired many times against a deadline.

The same shape appears outside seasonal work: ongoing high-turnover roles where a team is permanently mid-campaign and never gets ahead of the queue.
