Tech scale-up

Two recruiters, twelve open roles, one quarter

The situation

A Series-A company has twelve roles open for the quarter — backend and platform engineers, a designer, two account executives, a customer success lead — and two in-house recruiters covering all of it.

Roughly 40 applicants per role is manageable on paper. In practice the first round is the first thing to get compressed: a ten-minute call that was meant to probe depth becomes a scheduling conversation, and hiring managers end up doing the real screening themselves in interviews that were supposed to be technical deep-dives.

The symptom everyone recognises: an engineering manager two interviews into a loop asking why this candidate is here at all.

How the role is set up

Each role gets its own framework, generated from its job description and then reviewed — which turns out to be the more valuable half of the exercise.

  • The hiring manager reviews the extracted competencies before the role goes live. Disagreements about what the role actually needs surface in week one rather than in week five.
  • Every applicant gets the same first round — no more triage by whoever had a gap in their calendar.
  • Interviews are fixed-length by construction, so an engineering screen and a sales screen are each internally consistent.
  • Managers read scorecards before they agree to a loop, with each score linked to what the candidate actually said.
The framework review is the part teams underestimate. Most bad first rounds are really disagreements about the role, discovered late.

What changes for the team

  • Hiring managers stop doing screening in disguise. Technical loops go back to being technical, because the basics are already evidenced.
  • Twelve different roles run on one consistent method, which makes it possible to compare how hiring is going across the whole quarter rather than role by role.
  • Recruiters spend their hours on sourcing and closing — the two activities that a scale-up cannot automate and cannot afford to neglect.
  • Rejected candidates get a real reason, which matters more at this size, where every candidate is also a potential referrer or future applicant.

Where this shape of problem shows up

Lean in-house teams hiring across many different roles at once: Series-A to Series-C companies, agencies and consultancies staffing multiple client engagements, and any team where the recruiter-to-open-role ratio is worse than one to five.

It is least useful for the one or two most senior hires in that same quarter — a founding-team or VP search is a small, bespoke pool, and that first conversation should be a founder's.
